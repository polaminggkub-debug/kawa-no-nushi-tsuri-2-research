# What gear does to a fight: measured with the frame-exact engine

Source: the supplied Japanese ROM (SHA-1 `c2103dd94e2a1a65a495fc02adc2e7d040f31212`) through the ported fight loop ([fight-model.md](fight-model.md)). The figures are in [`data/gear-effects.json`](../data/gear-effects.json), rebuilt by `scripts/build_gear_effects.mjs` and kept in sync by `npm run check:generated`. Fights are played with each fish's stored best plain rhythm ([simulator](fight-model.md#auto-finder-and-the-simulator-page), human reaction 8 frames) over a fixed sample of 400 random starts. Not tested on real hardware.

## Findings

**Gear decides the fight, in two independent ways.**

1. **It sets the starting fight value, which is the number of mistakes you may make.** The meter starts at 0, 1, 3, 7, 15, 31 or 63 and every wasted beat climbs it one step (0, 1, 3, 7, 15, 31, 63); at 63 the fish is lost. Start 3 allows 4 steps (one reeling spell can waste 3 beats and live), start 7 allows 3, start 15 allows 2, start 63 none. *m* ("mistakes allowed") is that step count: m = 6 for start 0, 5 for 1, 4 for 3, 3 for 7, 2 for 15, 1 for 31, 0 for 63.
2. **It sets how far the fish may run before the line breaks.** The rod's *reach* times 336 is the range line. A fish dragged past it while the meter is full takes your tackle. Short rods lose big fish regardless of the start value.

**How big the effect is.**

* **The cliff is between start 7 and start 15.** Take the fish-and-method pairs (float and casting) that the best kit lands in 90 % or more of fights. Playing them at a reachable start of 0, 1 or 3 lands them 90 % or more in 100 %, 100 % and 94 % of pairs; at start 7 in 63 % of pairs (median 96.5 %); at start 15 in 18 % (median 1.5 %). 83 of the 102 pairs are landed in 95 % or more of fights with the best kit; for the rest tackle loss and slow fights cap the result.
* **Yamame (fish 3, 30..35 cm):** hooks that start it at 3 land it in 100 % of 400 fights, hooks that start it at 7 in 6 % (rod 16, the casting rod of the first measurement; on the best float rod, 100 % against 13.75 %). The worst legal Yamame kit starts at 7, the best at 0.
* **Giant eel (fish 59, 220 cm):** the best any kit can do is start 7 (m = 3). On rod 8: start 7 lands 11.5 % (14.3 % lose tackle, 74.3 % are still running at the 6000-frame cap), start 15 lands 0.5 %, start 31 lands 0 %. Rod reach is as decisive: at start 7, reach 3..9 lands 0 to 4.5 % and loses tackle in 94 to 100 %, reach 12 loses it in 85 %, reach 24 in 14 %.
* **Koi (fish 13):** at its best start the reach-24 rod lands 31 % and loses tackle in 6 %; reach 12 lands 14.5 % and loses 83 %; reach 3 lands nothing. Catfish (38) needs reach 15 or more, Herabuna (37) 15 or more, Akame (55) 24.
* **Across all kits:** the worst legal kit gives on average 3.2 fewer mistakes than the best for float fish (44 of 72 fish lose 3 or more), 3.1 for casting, 2.7 for fly, 2.2 for lure.

**Per slot** (one line each; per-fish numbers are in the JSON, `items[kind][id].use[method]`).

* **Rod.** Four things: the selector, the fish-match, the reach, the price. *Selector 0* (rods 1, 6, 10, 11, 14, 17) halves the start, then adds raises of the base by size (0, 1, 2 for sizes up to 15, 35 and above); it is best-in-slot for 56 of 72 float fish (rod 1) yet its reach is short. *Selector 1* (2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 9, 12, 15, 18, 20, 21) does nothing at first and adds 1, 0, 1 raises. *Selector 2* (8, 13, 16, 19) raises the start first and adds 2, 1, 0 raises, so it costs the most mistakes (mean 1.0 to 1.7 lost; bad for 46 of 72 float fish with rod 8) but has the longest reach. A rod whose fish-match equals the fish skips the size raises. Reach decides big fish: of the 72 float fish, 34 need more reach than float rods 1..6 and 7 offer (up to 9), 25 more than rod 9's 12, 15 more than rod 20's 15. Rods 2, 6, 11 and 17 are sold nowhere; rods 1, 8, 13, 16 only at the fixed-rod merchants (areas 5, 5, 4, 6).
* **Hook.** The hook is as strong a lever as the rod (mean best-to-worst gap 1.3 mistakes against 1.5 for float). A matching hook (fish id on the hook) halves the start. Otherwise the hook selector reacts to the fish size: selector 0 halves fish up to 15 cm and raises fish above 35 cm, selector 1 halves fish of 16..35 cm, selector 2 raises fish up to 15 cm and halves fish above 35 cm; hook 10 (selector 3) does nothing unless it matches Ayu. The cheap selector-0 hooks 7, 8 and 11 are the bad choice (mean 1.0 mistake lost, bad for 41 of 72 float fish); the cheapest good hook is hook 9 at 10 yen (selector 1, best for 40 fish, bad for 28). No hook is best for everything.
* **Bait.** Bait never lowers the start by itself: a bait whose fish-match equals the fish halves it exactly like a matching hook, and the two do not stack. Baits 3, 14, 15, 16, 17, 20 (and decoy ayu 23, not ported) are the only ones with a match. For choosing what bites, only the acceptance masks matter ([fish-acceptance-research.md](fish-acceptance-research.md)). In casting the sinker route admits only 30 of the 72 fish.
* **Float and sinker.** No effect on the fight (see Checks). Floats 1..8 and sinkers 9, 10 only choose the route (float rods use floats, casting rods sinkers); the cheapest are float 2 (10 yen) and sinker 10 (30 yen).
* **Lure.** The lure's own selector (0, 1, 2 by size, same table as hooks) sets the start; the three lure fish-matches (18 for 11, 33 for 38, 81 for 55) change nothing for real fish (see Checks). Selector-2 lures (38 of 81) cost 0.2 mistakes on average, selector-1 (37) 0.8, selector-0 (6) 1.3. The lure fight has an extra chase and strike stage that the simulator does not cover, so catch shares are not given for lures.
* **Fly (body only).** The body's selector (0 or 1) sets the start; the wing and tail do not enter the fight. Selector-1 bodies (17 of 64) lose 0.1 mistakes on average, selector-0 bodies (47) 0.7 to 0.8. The body's flag (0 or 1) only changes where the fish starts. Of the 64 bodies, 44 are in a shop bundle; the maker may offer the others, with prices not modelled here.

**Cheapest kits.** `buy` is the cheapest purchasable kit with the highest mistake score, restricted to rods whose reach reaches the fish's need; `buyAny` is the same ignoring reach (it differs for 42 of 173 fish-method pairs); `enough` is the cheapest kit with the right reach whose simulated catch share is within 3 points of the best; `cheap` is the cheapest kit overall.

**After the landing.** A caught fish can still cost the tackle. The fight loop ends, then a separate roll can break the hook, lure or fly (`01:84B7` into `01:8613`): 6.25 % for a hook or lure and 12.5 % for a fly at Jizo value 0, roughly half of that at 255 (figures from a separate audit of the ROM, not re-measured here). 5 of the 26 caught fights in `data/fight-traces` show the hook cleared afterwards (`ayu-r7-h10-reel`, `hera-r9-h5-b14-reel`, `nat-yamame-policy-careful`, `nat-yamame-policy-reel`, `yamame-r5-h13-reel`). A stack of spare hooks is therefore still needed after good gear.

## Checks

* **The two measurements reproduce exactly** (400 starts, seed 7102026, the first 400 of the simulator's final sample): Yamame on rod 16, every hook giving start 3 lands 100 % and every hook giving start 7 lands 6 %. Giant eel on rod 8, hooks giving start 7 land 11.5 %, start 15 land 0.5 %, start 31 land 0 %. With a different seed the eel start-7 figure moves between 11.5 and 18.8 % and start 15 between 0.5 and 2.5 %.
* **Floats and sinkers do not enter the fight.** `createFight` takes no float field and `resolveRecords` reads rod, fish, hook and bait (or lure, or fly). In the real ROM the float record loads to `$1250..$125E` (`03:D17D`); a scan of absolute-address operands finds no read of those addresses inside the fight code (`04:8000..B7FF`): the only bank-04 readers are the equipment set-up (`04:D2EB..D4D3`) and the float indicator and bite code (`04:E5C8..E86B`). Running the real ROM in the 65816 interpreter, 28 fights (four rod, fish and hook set-ups, float ids 1, 2, 4, 5, 8 and sinker ids 9, 10) gave frame-for-frame identical variables whichever float or sinker was written to RAM. The scan covers absolute operands only; long and indirect reads are not excluded by it, the interpreter run is the stronger evidence.
* **Fly selector matters; lure fish-match does not.** The fly selector rule (selector 0 `[halve, -, raise]`, selector 1 `[-, halve, -]` by size band, no fish-match) agrees with the ROM's starting value in 28 interpreter captures across bodies 1, 24, 43, 62 and all three size bands (14 with selector 1, 12 with fish of 15 cm or less). The lure fish-match rule agrees with the ROM in 6 of 15 lure captures, but it cannot change an outcome: lures 18, 33 and 81 are selector 2, and their fish (50..83, 60..100 and 120..150 cm) are all above 35 cm, where selector 2 halves anyway. The bait fish-match (15 captures) and hook selector 3 (2) agree with the ROM as well: all 62 captured starting values equal the engine's.
* **Scoring against the engine.** `scripts/gear-effects/engine-check.mjs` compares the closed-form kit scoring with `createFight(...).vars().fightBase` for about 300 random kits (927 start values, all methods and size bands); it runs in the build and in `check:generated`.

## Method

1. **Starting value.** For each fightable fish (72 ROM profiles, profile 67 is an empty placeholder), method and legal kit, the starting value is the engine's own routine (`04:8D2A..8EC1`) at one size per band (15, 35, 36 cm); m counts the raises to 63. Legal kits: float rods 1..9, 20, 21 with any of the 13 hooks and a bait that passes the fish's float-route gate (mode 0, nonzero threshold); casting rods 14..16 with any hook and a bait passing the sinker-route gate (mode 1, which also needs the profile's byte 13 bit 8 and a nonzero threshold); lure rods 10..13 with a lure passing the lure mask; fly rods 17..19 with one of the 64 ordinary bodies passing the body mask. Decoy ayu is left out. Masks only: bites also depend on position, random thresholds and, for flies, the hidden body/wing residue check.
2. **Per-item values.** The m an item "can still reach" is the best over every completion of the other slots, per size band. Loss against the best is the size-weighted mean gap; "best" means loss 0, "bad" loss of 1 or more.
3. **Purchasable kits.** Items must be on sale in at least one of the six areas (`data/shop-stock-rom.json`, prices from `data/item-table-records.json`; fly bodies by their ready-made bundle price). The float or sinker needed for the route is not part of the price (`routes` in the JSON).
4. **Catch share by start value.** For each fish and for float and casting, the stored best plain rhythm is played on the best-reach rod (the same rod the stored policy uses) with the starting value forced to each reachable value (the fish is given a profile start that no rod or hook changes), 400 starts, 6000-frame cap. Counts are caught, escaped, tackle lost, still running.
5. **Reach.** The same rhythm is played once per rod reach at the fish's best start value in its middle size band; each rod uses its own cast range, as in the simulator. The fish "needs" the shortest reach whose tackle-lost count is within 5 points of the longest rod's. Lures and flies borrow the need from float or casting because the range line and the fish's runs are the same in every method; this is an inference, not simulated.
6. **Limits.** The rhythm was tuned for the best kit and is reused for worse starts. "Still running" fights at the cap (74 % for the eel) are not failures; they are fights the policy had not finished in about 100 seconds. Lure and fly catch shares are not simulated. Prices of maker-only flies are not modelled.

## Statements elsewhere that this supersedes

Documents are left as written. These statements are now outdated:

* [hook-float-use.md](hook-float-use.md): "does **not** prove a higher bite rate, an easier catch" (line 7), and the Limits section's "there is no end-to-end controlled catch experiment" and "do not read ... as proof of ... catch probability". A matching hook halves the start; the effect on landing is measured above (still not a bite-rate effect). The float and sinker statements stand and are now confirmed to have no fight effect.
* [hook-practical-research.md](hook-practical-research.md): "The ROM does not provide enough information to rank every complete rod/bait/hook setup", "the ROM trace cannot recommend one general hook as universally better", "A half-sized value does **not** reliably delay this check" and the budget table's "do not rank landing success". The fight resets the meter to the start value after every rest, so the start value in the table above is what counts: halving never costs a mistake, selector-0 hooks 7, 8, 11 are the worst, hook 9 is the cheapest good one. The remark that the size field "is not a length in centimetres" is also outdated: the fight model uses the fish size in cm (validated against the traces).
* [rod-response-research.md](rod-response-research.md): "No consistent good/bad direction has been demonstrated for codes 0, 1, and 2", "not evidence of a stronger rod", "higher +3 ... not a measured universal catch-rate increase" and the "Rods that lead on traced fields ... not a tested best gear ranking" table. Selector 0 helps, selector 2 hurts, reach decides tackle loss for big fish; the table of leading rods is replaced by the per-fish kits.
* [fly-practical-research.md](fly-practical-research.md) and [fly-selection-practical-research.md](fly-selection-practical-research.md): "The traced code does not prove that either branch is easier or lands more fish" (body +2 and +6), "If a fish appears on both, the ROM evidence does not establish which body makes it bite more often" (the bite claim stands, the fight does not: selector-1 bodies start lower for most fish) and the cheapest-backup-set logic, which picks only on price and the hidden residue. The wing and tail statements stand: neither enters the fight. Body flag `+2` changes where the fish starts, not the meter.
* [bait-lure-player-choices.md](bait-lure-player-choices.md): "does not establish ... a fight advantage" (accepted for bites, but bait fish-matches and lure selectors do change the fight start), "A lure's fish-specific response fields are excluded from purchase recommendations because their gameplay effect is not established as a player advantage" (selectors 0, 1 and 2 differ by about 1 mistake on average) and the equal-price comparison ("not ... fight advantage"). The fish-match field on a lure is immaterial, the selector is not.
* The other `docs/fly-maker-*.md` pages only say that menu positions do not rank catching; that stays true.
* [fight-model.md](fight-model.md): "a cleared hook only for tackle loss" is corrected there.
