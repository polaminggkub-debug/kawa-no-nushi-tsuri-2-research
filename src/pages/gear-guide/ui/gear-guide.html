How to choose your gear

Pick your fish and get the gear to buy. Short steps first; the reasons are folded away under “Why”.

Pick your gear in 5 steps

  1. Pick your fish

    Find it in the table below (type its name). Every fish has one best kit.

  2. Check how big it is

    Real fish sizes start at half the notebook minimum and go up to the MAX size. Small is up to 15 cm, medium 16–35 cm, big over 35 cm. The table shows each fish’s real size.

  3. Pick the rod

    Best: the rod named for the fish (for example the Yamame rod for Yamame). If there is none: a light rod for small fish ({{name:rod:6}}, {{name:rod:14}}), a light or medium rod for medium fish, and any rod for big fish. A heavy rod ({{name:rod:8}}) never makes the fight easier; buy one only when the fish needs its reach.

  4. Pick the hook and the bait

    A hook or a bait named for the fish makes the fight one step easier (they do not add up). If none is named, match the hook to the size: small ({{name:hook:8}}, {{name:hook:7}}, {{name:hook:11}}) up to 15 cm, medium ({{name:hook:9}}, {{name:hook:6}}) for 16–35 cm, big ({{name:hook:4}}, {{name:hook:3}}, {{name:hook:1}}) over 35 cm. The bait only has to be one the fish takes; the table gives the cheapest.

  5. Pick the method and the float

    A float rod with bait catches all 72 fish, so start there. Use the lure or fly tabs only if you want to try them: 38 fish take lures and 33 take flies. Floats and sinkers never change the fight; they only decide which fish can bite.

A good kit makes the fight start easier: the fish has to pull more times before it bolts and gets away. The boxes show it: more filled = easier.

Search a fish. Each row is the kit that makes the fight start easiest for the sizes that fish really has.

Why: the proof from the ROM
  • Gear sets how hard a fight starts, on a scale from 0 (easiest) to 6 (hardest). One level lower gives the fish one more pull before it bolts. Proven in a running game: with start 3, 2 and 0 holding A loses the fish at frame 12, 28 and 92.
  • Three things set the start: the rod (light is one easier, heavy one harder, then a penalty when the rod is the wrong size for the fish unless it is named for it), then one of the hook, bait, lure or fly body (named for the fish: one easier; otherwise by size), and the fish itself.
  • Real fish size runs from half the record minimum up to the MAX size, so 34 of the 72 fish reach a smaller size band than the notebook suggests (Yamame is 15–35 cm, not 30–35).
  • A fish only bites an item it accepts: bait, lure or fly body. Time, weather, rod and hook do not change who bites.
  • Not proven: how much rod reach each fish needs. The table uses the wiki’s own simulation for that, so treat it as an estimate. The share of fish you land also depends on your rhythm.

How we know

The numbers come from the original game's own code and from a port of its fight loop that matches real emulator runs frame by frame. Mistakes allowed and landing rates are simulated: 400 fights per case with the best A rhythm, a reaction time of 8 frames. They are not a promise for any single fight and were not tested on real hardware.

Which fish bite which bait, lure or fly comes from the game's tables and was checked in the emulator: a fish on the exact tile bit every time the item was on its list. The fly lock was measured from a blank save; a first boot on real hardware was not tested. Prices and shops were read from all six shops, the three special rod merchants and the fly makers in the real game.
