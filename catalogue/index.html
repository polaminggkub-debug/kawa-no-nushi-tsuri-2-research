What to buy, carry and do before fishing

Lure / casting: restore HP to 100 first to get your rod’s full aiming time.

This restores aiming time; a bite-rate bonus is not established.

Float fishing: which rod should I buy first?

In area 1, choose the clear-stream carbon rod, 5.3 m (04), for ¥500: it gives more aim time and more fish-position room before the traced hook-loss branch than rod 03 at ¥350. Rod 05 costs ¥750 but does not give more aim time than 04. In area 3, if you want the longest aim window among float rods, when choosing a new rod, the 10 m carbon Ayu rod (14) costs ¥1,500—the same full price as rod 07—and gives more aiming time and fish-position room before that branch.

A longer aim window gives you more time to move the target tile. A higher fish-position threshold gives the fish more room before one traced branch can take away the hook.

Clear stream carbon rod, 5.3 mAyu carbon rod, 10 m
Area 1 stocks 03/04/05; area 3 stocks 07/14. This does not rank bite or landing odds, and fish have other escape routes.

Lure fishing: where is the useful upgrade?

In area 1, when buying a rod, choose the large lure rod (0C) at its full ¥600 price instead of the small rod (0A) at ¥500 if you can afford the extra ¥100. If you already own 0A and its aim time is enough, keep using it. In area 4, the heavy-fish lure rod (0D) costs ¥650 and has the longest lure-rod aim window and gives the most fish-position room before the traced lost-lure branch. Buy it for those two effects, not on the expectation of easier bites.

Rod 0C gives more aim time than 0A; 0D extends aim time and gives more fish-position room before the traced lost-lure branch.

Casting: start cheap, then upgrade if aim time matters

The small casting rod (0E) is the ¥250 entry option in area 1. If you need more time to adjust your cast, skip to the medium rod (0F) for ¥600 in area 2. The two-handed rod (10) at ¥1,500 in area 6 has the longest aim window in this style, but it is optional if the medium rod is enough for you.

The medium rod gives more time to adjust the target than the small one; the two-handed rod gives the most in the traced casting line.

The listed rods are stocked in areas 1/2/6 respectively. Upgrade when the extra aiming time matters to your play; this is not a catch-rate ranking.

Fly fishing: should I buy the large rod directly?

If you plan to fly-fish and are choosing a new rod, buy the large fly rod (13) for its full ¥450 price in area 1. The medium rod's full price is ¥400; the large rod gives the most aim time and fish-position room before the traced lost-fly branch. This is not a ¥50 trade-in or upgrade price if you already own the medium rod.

The large rod costs ¥450 and the medium ¥400; the measured differences affect target-adjustment time and this one escape boundary.

Want a two-lure kit? Carry this pair

Starting a new kit in area 1: buy Spoon 2E for ¥25 and Soft worm 23 for ¥30, ¥55 total. Starting a new kit in area 4: buy Sinking lure 17 for ¥20 with Soft worm 23, ¥50 total. If you already own the Spoon-and-worm pair, buying Sinking does not save ¥5.

This pair passes the ROM lure-compatibility check for all 38 profiles in the lure table, so you can cover that verified set with two items.

Fly choice: match the body to the fish; don’t chase a ‘best’ wing

If your target appears in the wet-body fish list, area 1 sells a ready-made bundle—body 01, wing 09, tail 13—for ¥5. If the target is absent from the 33 wet-body profiles, use compatible bait or another fishing style; no ordinary fly body is confirmed for that profile. No wing or tail is proven to improve catches.

Low HP? Restore it before fishing and buy only what you need

Before lure or casting fishing, restore HP to 100: below 100 HP the game shortens the aim window for those two styles. All shop foods cost ¥1 per HP. For the most recovery from one serving, choose the 40-HP bento (06) for ¥40, stocked from area 5. To reach 100, choose one food or combine servings for at least your missing HP: area 1 orange 01 restores 5 for ¥5 or rice ball 02 restores 10 for ¥10; area 2 dango 03 restores 15 for ¥15; area 3 banana 04 restores 20 for ¥20; area 4 grapes 05 restores 30 for ¥30. Any recovery beyond maximum HP is wasted.

At 100 HP, the rod has its full aim window. Recovery beyond missing HP is wasted, and the game refuses food when HP is full. Milk restores HP to its maximum and can be refilled at the area 3 cow, but save it if you still want to trade for the canoe.

Quest items: trade the milk before drinking and check the key chests

Area 3: take the bottle from the town chest (6,4), fill it at the cow (6,103), then trade the milk to the canoe maker (28,39) before drinking it. Drinking restores full HP but leaves an empty bottle. Key 17 opens town chests in areas 1/2/4/6 and is retained; leave room for the reward in the matching inventory. Area 4: give fried tofu 15 to the NPC at (32,42) for the fox-story step, then use fireworks 16; fireworks can also trigger the fox scene directly at X31–33, Y42–43. In area 6, give candle 12 to the NPC at (47,36).

Drinking milk turns it back into an empty bottle, so it cannot then be traded for the canoe. The key is not consumed, but each chest needs room for its reward.

These locations and outcomes come from ROM chest/NPC branches. Direct fireworks use and the tofu story step are separate routes.

Do not buy a hook just because its name matches a fish

Choose a compatible bait and fishing style for the target first. Do not pay extra for a fish-matched hook expecting easier bites or catches.

A fish-matched hook changes the fight timing after a bite and can move the later escape/lost-hook check earlier or later. It does not add bait compatibility.

No hook is proven to land fish more easily in every situation. Fish-specific response details remain in the technical evidence.

HOW TO READ THIS

Confirmed, translated, and still unknown

The catalogue contains all 315 named/listed entries: 21 rods, 81 lures, 64 fly-body records, 47 wing records, 23 tail records, 23 baits, 13 hooks, 10 floats/sinkers, 10 foods, and 23 tools/quest items. The 64 bodies + 47 wings + 23 tails account for all 134 fly-table records.

All 134 ROM fly-table records are represented here as 64 bodies, 47 wings, and 23 tails. ID 87 is a no-item code and is excluded.

ROM price fields are not proof of shop stock. A zero field does not establish a free item. Fly-table price values are treated as component fields, not finished-fly retail prices.

The custom fly maker was directly observed in the first-stage shop. Captured sequence: choose family, body, wing, tail or none, then confirm. The observed Mayfly palette had 20 wing choices and 9 tail sprites plus a separate None option. One measured order cost 25 yen (5 + 5 + 15); this quote is not a universal price for all combinations.

The screenshots came from the user-supplied unpatched Japanese ROM in an isolated Snes9x runtime. For hidden items, valid inventory IDs were temporarily written into WRAM so the game itself rendered the selected item. No AI-generated art was used.
